Flight Delay Compensation Guide

Learn what details to collect after a disrupted flight and where to verify possible eligibility. No outcome is guaranteed.

Start with the rules that apply to your itinerary

Passenger rights vary by departure country, arrival country, operating airline, disruption type, timing, notice, and cause. A long delay does not by itself guarantee payment. This page is general information, not legal advice.

Situations worth investigating

  • A flight arrived substantially later than scheduled.
  • An airline cancelled a flight or denied boarding.
  • A missed connection on a single booking caused a much later final arrival.
  • The disruption involved a route covered by passenger-protection rules.

Eligibility may depend on circumstances within or outside an airline’s control. Weather, air-traffic restrictions, security events, strikes, technical issues, crew matters, and notice timing can be treated differently under different regimes.

Collect useful records

Keep the booking confirmation, ticket number, boarding pass, scheduled and actual times, written airline messages, receipts for reasonable expenses, and notes about what airline staff said. Record the operating airline as well as the seller. Do not publish documents containing personal information.

Verify with relevant sources

Check the operating airline’s official claims process and the aviation authority or consumer body for the jurisdictions connected to the flight. Use exact dates, airports, flight numbers, and arrival times. If you consider a third-party claims provider, review its fees, authorization terms, privacy policy, and cancellation rules before agreeing.

No guarantee: Road Trip Ledger cannot determine eligibility, interpret law for your case, or promise compensation. Deadlines and rights can change; verify current rules with the relevant authority or qualified adviser.
