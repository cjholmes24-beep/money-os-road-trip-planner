HOW TO PLAN BETTER

Use estimates as a buffer, not a promise

Real trips rarely match a perfect spreadsheet. Construction, detours, weather, idling, traffic, and extra local driving can increase fuel use. A small mileage buffer can make the estimate more realistic.

Your real-world MPG can differ from the dashboard rating because of speed, cargo weight, terrain, wind, tire pressure, and air-conditioning use. If you are unsure, use a slightly lower MPG number so the budget is less optimistic.

Lodging totals can also change because of taxes, parking charges, resort fees, or refundable deposits. Check the full price shown by the hotel before making a reservation.

For longer drives, plan rest stops and overnight breaks conservatively. Fatigue can turn a cheap trip into an unsafe one. Keep offline directions available when traveling through areas with weak cellular service.

FORMULAS

What the calculator is doing

Fuel cost: trip miles divided by MPG, multiplied by fuel price per gallon.

Hotel cost: nightly rate multiplied by number of hotel nights.

Food cost: food budget per person per day multiplied by travelers and travel days.

Total trip: fuel + hotels + food + miscellaneous expenses.

Per-person cost: total trip cost divided by number of travelers.

These are planning estimates only. Verify current fuel, lodging, toll, parking, food, and destination prices before spending money.
