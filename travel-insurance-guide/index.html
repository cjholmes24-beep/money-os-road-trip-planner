Travel Insurance Guide

Travel insurance is a contract, not a magic shield. Compare what is covered, what is excluded, the limits, and how a claim has to be documented.

Start with what you actually need protected

Common policy categories can include trip cancellation or interruption, emergency medical expenses, emergency evacuation, baggage loss or delay, and certain travel delays. Not every policy contains every category, and definitions, limits, deductibles, and exclusions can differ.

Read the details that change the value

  • Maximum benefit limits for each coverage category.
  • Deductibles, waiting periods, and documentation requirements.
  • Covered reasons for cancellation or interruption.
  • Pre-existing-condition rules and any waiver requirements.
  • Geographic limits, trip-length limits, and activity exclusions.
  • Whether reimbursement is primary or secondary to other coverage.

Compare policies using the same trip facts

Use the same travelers, dates, destination, trip cost, and coverage priorities when comparing policies. A lower premium can come with lower limits or narrower coverage, so price alone does not describe the contract.

Before buying

Read the certificate or policy wording supplied by the insurer. Confirm cancellation terms and know how to reach assistance while traveling. Keep receipts and written records relevant to a possible claim.
